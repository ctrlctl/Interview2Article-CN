## Q4 — Design Principles

**Host**: Are there any consistent goals or principles when you are designing your products?

**Guest B**: I think these three words really — the inspiring factor and keeping it musical. So we try to not design every single control from a technical perspective, but from a musical goal. So you have one knob and it doesn't need to be like setting analog capacitors anywhere. Most of our designs are digital, and even the analog ones, they have some macro controls, and there's always some musical intention behind every single control. So we think about pots more like musical controls, not like technical controls.

## Q7 — Neo Trinity Tips

**Host**: Dark Matter. So we are using Neo Trinity — what do you usually use? Four and other advanced tips for using that?

**Guest B**: With Neo Trinity, we were rethinking what modulation is, and how you would want to do complex modulation from a musical perspective. So there are three different ways of creating modulation, and they all have this gestural control that you can record the knob for, and then it keeps repeating itself. So it's sort of like a looper but for modulation. I think this is a very direct musical way of expressing yourself and translating the musical idea into the sound.

## Q8 — Kastle Series Origin

**Host**: Recently the Kastle series — like the standalone one — are really popular among the YouTube channels and also in China. And also this Saturday — we just released last month [Kastle 2]. How did you come up with the idea of doing such a series of products?

**Guest B**: It started many years ago when I was designing the first Kastle series — that was like 10 years ago. It came up with the idea to make modular synths powered by batteries, as compact as possible. And also cheap and affordable for everyone — because modular can be expensive. Yes, there was the first Kastle series which had three modules — synthesizer, drum machine, and also an arpeggio note-playing machine. So the Kastle 2 is like a reworked first Kastle series, with more sound fidelity and more sonic possibilities. So that's our favorite product because it's approachable even by beginners, but also people use it in their live concerts as well. So even though it's small but very versatile, you can also use it for big shows.

## Q9 — Next Kastle Firmware: The Alchemist

**Host**: Since they are a platform, as you mentioned, you can switch firmware using any USB cable. What would be the next firmware for the Kastle series? Can we have a little bit of a spoiler about it?

**Guest B**: Yeah, it's here. There's a prototype about the new firmware which is called the Alchemist.

**Host**: So what is the Alchemist?

**Guest B**: The Alchemist is a synthesizer firmware — there are five different synthesis modes that are very flexible but also very musical. And I think they complement each other well, and what I like is that you can also modulate between the different modes. You can control subtractive synthesis with FM synthesis and it becomes very natural — but it would be quite hard to do it otherwise. You would need a lot of modular gear to be able to do these kinds of sounds and transitions. But here, I think it takes over a decade of our experience in modular synths and boils it down into something that is just instantly fun. But there is more than a decade of work and experience that went into making that.

## Q10 — Beginner Tips

**Host**: There are a lot of questions from beginners — when we want to start with Eurorack first, it's expensive and also it's hard to build something from scratch. Are there any beginner tips?

**Guest B**: I think it's great to start with a semi-modular, because semi-modulars typically have everything you need to start. And if you expand from a semi-modular, I think that's a great way to start. It would be easier to have something pre-built than to have the ability to patch it to something else.

---

## Q11 — One Product to Start a Rack

**Host**: So if you have to start your rack from scratch, and you can only buy one product as the first one from Bastl Instruments, which would you pick?

**Guest B**: That one is easy — it would be the Softpop [SP2 / wave pad]. Because there's a sound source — you can put your own sound, so you can already start working with some sound aesthetic that you choose. And it gives you a lot of exploration — it has MIDI input, it has headphone output, so it has everything you need to connect with your other gear. And it's fun. Also, if you expand with one, two, three modules from that — it has quite powerful modulation sources, so you can bring the system together from the very start. And if you expand from that, I can guarantee it will be fun. Because it might be quite frustrating when you're starting, if you get a VCO and a filter — yeah, that's just a mono sound. It takes a while — you need quite some modules to start to have fun — but with this one, it's fun from the start. It's cheap, it's fun, and you can enjoy all those modulations and interesting parts of modular. It combines a lot of modules together which you would otherwise need to get separately. Maybe if you want deep modulations, you can get the Neo Trinity and similar products, but the Softpop can get you started definitely.
